Your first clients come for what you know

A new real estate agent in Panama gets their first clients through reputation: knowing one area in depth, answering quickly with facts and writing down what they know.

Message me on WhatsApp

"Pay for ads." "Join every group."

That's advice a new agent hears a lot. It may bring in messages. What it rarely brings is someone who comes back, or who recommends you.

How does a new real estate agent get clients in Panama?

A new real estate agent in Panama gets their first clients more through reputation than advertising: knowing one area or property type in depth, answering quickly and with facts, keeping their word with other agents and writing down what they know. It's slow at first. What works builds up over the years.

Why specialize in one area?

Knowing "Panama" is no use to anyone. Knowing twenty buildings in one corregimiento (a city district) is: what the monthly fee is in each, which ones allow nightly rentals, which has had its pool closed for months, which units face the noisy side. The same goes for a property type: small retail spaces, beach houses, apartments for first-time buyers.

What you're after is a long game: relationships that last years, where trust grows over time, on a board small enough to know completely. When someone asks about that piece of the market, you answer in detail, and someone who doesn't know it answers in generalities. It shows in the first message.

Answer fast, but not just anything

Speed matters. Someone who writes at nine at night and gets a reply at ten past nine already knows something about you.

But a quick "hi, of course" is worth less than a reply that takes twenty minutes and says: "That apartment has a high fee for what the building offers, and from the twelfth floor up the view is blocked by the tower next door. Still interested, or should I look for something else?" The client cares less about the time than about whether you saved them a wasted visit.

Keep your word with other agents

A large part of the work runs through working with other agents: one brings the client, the other has the property. Done well, it spares the client the mess of working with many agents. That's also where your reputation gets made, and it gets made fast, because the same people keep crossing paths.

Answer the searches other agents share even when you have nothing; a "I don't have one, but I saw something similar in such-and-such building" gets remembered. Show up on time for viewings. Don't go behind another agent's back to their client.

Does writing help you get clients?

Anywhere others can find it will do; you don't need a blog: a note on what to check in a PH's bylaws (PH, or propiedad horizontal, is Panama's condo regime, with shared common areas and a monthly fee), an explanation of why two units in the same building aren't worth the same.

Writing forces you to think more clearly and to separate what you read from what you saw on the job, which is the gap between learning and operating. And someone who reads you before writing to you arrives with some trust already earned.

The first month

For the first month, something like this is enough: pick one area or property type and visit ten buildings, noting the fee, the state of the common areas and what's being listed in each; put a follow-up date on the calendar for every person who wrote; and at the end of the month, publish a short note on something learned on those visits.

No client shows up the following week because of it. But it starts to add up, and when the first one arrives, it's almost always because of something you already knew about that building.

Frequently asked questions

What if the area I picked barely moves?

That gives you time to know it better than anyone before it does. If after a few months you see no deals, widen to the neighboring area rather than jumping to the other side of the city.

How can I tell if an agent really knows the area?

Ask something that isn't in the listing: what the monthly fee is in that building, whether the common areas are working, which units face the noisy side. Someone who knows the area answers in detail or tells you they'll find out. Someone who doesn't answers in generalities.
