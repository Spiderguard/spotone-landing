Ask an agent for a method, not a number

To choose a listing agent in Panama, check their license and ask for their pricing method and comparables: how they answer says more than the price they promise.

Message me on WhatsApp

Why did you pick the last agent you spoke to? If the honest answer is that they gave you the highest number, or that you liked them, it's worth rethinking before your property goes on the market.

Both matter, but neither tells you how they'll work. And what happens to a property in its first weeks on the market tends to shape everything that follows.

How do you choose a real estate agent to sell your home in Panama?

To choose an agent to sell your home in Panama, first confirm they hold a license in their own name. Then ask for their pricing method, the comparables they use, how they'll present and market the property, how they work with other agents and how often they'll report back. How they answer matters more than the price they promise.

What should you ask an agent before choosing them?

The first one isn't up for debate: the license has to be in the agent's own name, not just the company's. Checking takes a few minutes (here's how). After that come the requests that actually set one agent apart from another.

  • Their pricing method. A range, and the reasons behind it, because a price without a method is just a rumor. An agent with a method can tell you what would push your price up or down within that range. The highest number at the first meeting is easy to say and hard to defend once viewings start.
  • Comparables from the same building or the same floor plan; for a house, the same model in the same development. Ask which ones sold, which are still listed and for how long, and where each figure comes from: a closing they know about or an asking price. An apartment in another tower with a different layout says little about yours, even with the same square meters.
  • How they'll present it. Ask to see a listing they've written and notice the order in which it tells the story of the property: whether it explains who the place makes sense for, or just counts bedrooms and bathrooms. Look at the photos the same way, because a good property with bad photos looks like something else.
  • Where they'll market it. Portals, their network of other agents, buyers they already have looking for something similar, and whether they plan an open house and for whom. The concrete version of that question would be: "Who are you sending it to in the first week?"
  • How they work with other agents. A sale is often done as a co-broke, with one agent on each side of the deal. What matters to you is that the property circulates at one price, with one version of the listing, and doesn't end up listed by everyone and worked by no one.
  • How often and how they'll report to you. How many inquiries, how many viewings and, above all, what the people who saw it and didn't make an offer said. That feedback is worth more than any view count, because it tells you what buyers see and you've stopped noticing.

How do you read their answers?

You're looking for concrete answers, even imperfect ones. The agent who shows you comparables, even when they lower your expectations, respects you more than the one who only talks about how fast it will sell.

If the agent with a method gives you a lower number, ask what would have to happen to reach the higher one. If there's no answer, the higher number didn't have one either.

Pay attention to what they ask you, too: why you're selling, when you need the money, what you'd do if no buyer shows up in a few months. Without that, what you get is one more listing on a portal.

None of these questions is a test meant to make anyone uncomfortable. A good agent welcomes them, because they organize the conversation from day one, and they let you see, before you sign anything, whether you're being offered a method or just a number.

Frequently asked questions

Should the agent see the property before giving me a price?

Ideally, yes. A price over the phone works as a reference, but condition, view, light and noise all move the range, and you only see those in person.
