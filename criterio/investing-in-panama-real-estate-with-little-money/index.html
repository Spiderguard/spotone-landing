Before the property, build a track record

Investing in Panama real estate with little money starts before you buy: provable income, steady savings, a clean credit file and knowing one area well.

Message me on WhatsApp

"I want to invest in real estate, but I don't have that much money. Where do I start?"

The question usually comes from younger people with a steady job and savings that don't stretch to anything on the listing sites. It's a good question, and the internet mostly answers it badly.

How do you start investing in Panama real estate with little money?

Investing in Panama real estate with little money starts before you buy: income you can document, steady savings, a clean credit file and real knowledge of one area. Without money of your own, there's no serious way to do it. The first purchase is usually your own home or a small unit in a building you already know.

Can you invest with no money of your own?

The videos that promise you can buy property without putting anything down leave out one detail: if the money isn't yours, it's someone else's, and it comes with conditions. It might be a bank, a partner or the seller financing the difference. None of those is bad. The problem is going in without knowing what happens if things turn out worse than planned.

With a small down payment, everything gets magnified. If the price rises a little, you look like a genius; if it drops by the same amount, a good chunk of what you put in is gone, and the mortgage payment still arrives every month. For how much to put down, see how much of a down payment to make, and if you plan to rent the place out, the full math is in buy to rent.

What does the bank look at in your record?

Banks don't lend on your plans. They lend on what comes in each month and how long it's been coming in, on how you've paid what you owe and on the savings you can show. The full checklist is in mortgage requirements in Panama. Almost all of it can be built with little money and a fair amount of time.

How you pay gets recorded at the APC, the credit bureau checked by banks, finance companies and stores that sell on credit. A small credit card paid on time for years carries more weight there than any well-explained plan. And saving works better when it doesn't depend on willpower: an automatic transfer on payday, into an account you don't touch. At first, the habit matters more than the amount.

One area, on foot

No listing site gives you this part. Pick an area. Just one. Walk it. Check that area's listings every week and write down the asking prices (which aren't the same as what things close for), how long listings stay up and which buildings keep showing up again and again.

After a year of that routine, a price that's out of line starts to jump out at you. That's a track record too: yours with the area. The bigger signals, the ones that suggest whether an area will rise, are in how to tell if an area is going to appreciate.

The first purchase

For most people, the first property is their own home, and that's fine, as long as you don't mistake it for an investment: buying to live in is not buying to invest.

Some people do the opposite: they keep renting where they live and buy a rental somewhere else. It can work if it passes two tests. First, you can pay your rent and the new mortgage at the same time, even if the unit sits empty for a month. Second, the numbers work with the rent the area actually gets for a long lease, even if that's less than you'd need.

What gets pitched most to people with little money is pre-construction with installments during the build. Before you sign, ask what happens if, at handover, the bank won't lend you the balance; buying pre-construction is betting on time, and you need to know how much.

Whatever you buy, make sure you can sell it: your first property is also bought for the way out.

This month

Start with the APC. Request your report this week: by law, the simple report is free, in person with your cédula (Panama's national ID), as often as you like; you pay only for the official certification (Law 24 of 2002, article 28, as amended by Law 14 of 2006, in Spanish). Read every line. Look for debts you don't recognize and payments you made that don't show up. If you've paid off a debt, the law requires it to be updated within three business days of the payment (Law 195 of 2020, in Spanish).

Walking the area can wait until Saturday.

None of this looks like investing, which is why so many people skip it. Before you buy your first property, build a track record.

Frequently asked questions

Can I start with a partner or a relative?

Yes, and it can work. Before you buy, put in writing who puts in what, who covers the monthly costs, how any income is split and how one of you gets out if the other wants to sell. What you agree on before buying heads off most of the fights that come later.
