What "línea blanca" actually includes in a Panama rental

Two out of three listings that use the term declare a fridge, stove, washer and dryer. One in five names no appliance at all.

Message me on WhatsApp

The tenant read "línea blanca completa" — full white goods. Moving day came: fridge and stove were there. The washer wasn't.

The listing said something imprecise. Each side understood something different.

"Línea blanca" is used constantly, but it has no uniform composition. So I went to see what the listings actually say.

Proprietary data

What the listings declare

I reviewed active residential rental listings on Encuentra24 Panama — apartments and houses, as of 12 August 2026. Of 14,250 listings, 4,061 use the term: more than one in four.

Two out of three declare the same four appliances: fridge, stove, washer and dryer. Four in ten declare exactly those four and nothing else.

Beyond that core of four, agreement drops. A microwave joins the core in one of every five listings. A dishwasher, in roughly one in seven. All six together appear in fewer than one in ten.

And at the other extreme, one in five writes "línea blanca" without naming a single appliance.

The term has no uniform composition — but it isn't empty either. The listings show a recognizable core of four appliances. The uncertainty sits at the edges: in the additional appliances, where agreement drops considerably, and in the listing that uses "línea blanca" without declaring any.

No published definition

What I couldn't find

I found no single published definition from MIVIOT, ACOBIR or Encuentra24 establishing which appliances the term must include in a rental (verified 12 August 2026). The same holds for "amoblado" (furnished) and "equipado" (equipped).

Panama's standard lease form doesn't mention appliances either: it speaks of the property and its installations.

These are commercial conventions. They mean whatever each party assumes — until someone writes it down.

Five layers worth separating

In a rental transaction, five distinct layers are worth separating:

  • What was advertised. The listing that made you call.
  • The pattern in the listings. The core that shows up most often — though not in all of them.
  • What was agreed. What ended up in the lease. That's the main reference for what the parties committed to hand over.
  • What was delivered. What was there on key day.
  • What should have been itemized. The inventory that should have been made.

The listing creates the expectation. The lease should define the obligation. The inventory proves what happened. When all three say the same thing, there's far less room to argue.

Recommendation

If you're renting

Treat those four as a starting reference, but confirm in writing every appliance included before you sign. The term on its own doesn't indicate which appliances are included.

Ask which appliances are included, with make and model where possible, and have it annexed to the lease. A written reply can serve as a record, though the cleanest route is folding it into the lease or its inventory.

Also ask who coordinates and pays for maintenance, repair and replacement. They are not the same obligation.

Prevention

If you're listing

Here's the uncomfortable part for our side of the counter: writing "línea blanca completa" without itemizing is convenient for the listing and expensive at handover.

And one detail makes it harder to justify: Encuentra24 already has checkboxes for fridge, stove, washer, dryer, microwave and dishwasher. The boxes are there, and ticking them takes seconds. In fact, nearly every listing that does specify does it there, not in the description.

Even so, one in five listings using the term ticks none.

A listing that says what's in and what's out filters better, wastes fewer viewings and reaches signing without surprises.

When this stops being the broker's job

If an appliance was advertised, wasn't delivered and the lease doesn't clarify it, the argument is about what was offered versus what was agreed — and that may need legal review. A broker can structure the conversation and document it; a broker can't decide who's right.

Two out of three listings declare the same core of four appliances. The term alone still does not tell you exactly what will be delivered.

Methodology. Proprietary data from SpotOne's database of Encuentra24 Panama listings. Cut-off: 12 August 2026. Universe: active listings for residential rentals (apartments and houses); furnished, commercial, office and room categories excluded. Counts are of listings, not unique properties — the same unit may be advertised by several brokers. Removing listings with identical title, price, area and bedroom count leaves the proportions intact (the core of four moves from 67.2% to 65.2%; those declaring nothing, from 21.4% to 23.3%). Title, description and the portal's structured features field were reviewed. The data measures what the listing declares, not what the property contains.
