You'll never have all the information

If you're afraid to buy an apartment, settle what can't be fixed before the purchase agreement: clean title, a monthly cost you can carry, loan approval and a reserve.

Message me on WhatsApp

"Let me look into it a bit more and I'll get back to you."

It's a common line from buyers who have seen the unit three times, who have the bank's approval and who know the monthly cost by heart. By then they already have what they need to decide. What they're waiting for is a fact that will decide for them, and that one doesn't come.

What should you do if you're afraid to buy an apartment in Panama?

If you're afraid to buy an apartment in Panama, you don't need to know everything to decide. You need what can't be fixed after you sign the purchase agreement: clean title, income that covers the monthly cost with room to spare, the loan approved in writing and a cash reserve. Everything else can be decided quickly or wait.

Why does fear always ask for one more fact?

Buying is expensive and hard to undo, so doubt has a good reputation. Asking for another viewing, another comparable, or waiting to see what rates do all looks responsible.

People who make a lot of decisions tend to share a rule: most things get decided with a good part of the information, not all of it, as long as you can correct course quickly if you got it wrong. In a purchase, correcting course doesn't cost the same at every stage. Until you sign the promesa de compraventa (the purchase agreement that sets price, deadlines and conditions before the deed), almost everything can be undone cheaply; if you paid a deposit to hold the unit, ask first under what conditions it's refunded. After the promesa, that's no longer true. So whatever can be undone is worth deciding quickly. The promesa deserves time.

What can't you skip before buying?

None of this gets skipped, however rushed the buyer is:

  • The paperwork. The finca (the property's record at the Registro Público, Panama's public registry) in the seller's name, with no liens that won't be cleared at closing. And the paz y salvo, the certificate showing the unit owes nothing to the building.
  • The monthly numbers. Mortgage payment, condo fee, property tax (if it applies) and insurance, against your real income. How to check them is in three numbers before you buy.
  • Confirmed financing, if you need it: the loan approval in writing, not a conversation with the loan officer. The carta promesa de pago (the bank's letter committing to pay the seller the loan amount once the sale is recorded at the Registro Público) is a separate document that comes at a different point; your bank will confirm the order.
  • A reserve that isn't your down payment.

If one of these is missing, waiting is the right call.

What can wait

The next move in interest rates. Nobody knows it precisely enough for waiting to give you an edge, and I went into that in is it a good time to buy?.

The cheaper listing that popped up last night in another building, with a different view and a different condo fee.

Your brother-in-law's take on "the market." He may be right about the market and know nothing about this finca.

And the fourth viewing at the same time of day. If you go back, go to see something you haven't seen yet: the building at night, when everyone is home, or the parking garage when it's full.

Two columns

One practical way out of the tangle is to split your list of doubts in two.

One column holds the ones you can fix after you buy: the kitchen, the wall color, the furniture, even the life plan if one day you move out and rent the place. Those doubts don't deserve another month of waiting.

The other holds the ones with no fix: a problem with the finca, a monthly cost you can't carry, a building whose owners' meetings never decide anything. That column is worth your time, and it's almost never where the doubt that freezes people lives.

Anything you still need to confirm after signing the promesa, such as the appraisal or the bank's final approval, ask your lawyer how to write it in as a condition of that agreement. And a good list of what to rule out helps from the start: what you rule out early, you no longer have to investigate.

Frequently asked questions

What if a better option shows up after I buy?

It will, because the market doesn't stop the day you sign. The fair comparison is with what you had in front of you when you decided, and with what you needed to solve.

Is it normal to feel scared right before signing?

Yes, and on its own it isn't a sign of anything. Use it to go back over the column of things you can't fix later. If that column is settled, the fear is telling you about the size of the decision.
