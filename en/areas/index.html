What this data does not say.

  • It does not measure closings or absorption. These are asking prices: a listing records no transaction.
  • Price per m² doesn’t account for condition, floor, view or age. It mixes new towers with buildings from the 1970s. Whether something is expensive or cheap is judged against the same floor plan in the same building.
  • Listings are duplicated. One apartment can carry dozens of listings from different advertisers; here it counts once. The count is an estimate: the real figure sits between units and listings.
  • Listed doesn’t mean available: a listing can stay up after the place is rented or sold.

Method.

  • Active: a listing published online in Panama as of the November 13, 2026 cut-off.
  • Area: the one the listing declares, without listings whose title names another area.
  • Type: by the listing title, not its category, which sometimes mixes houses with apartments.
  • Unit: same price, m² and bedrooms within the same area, deal type (sale or rent) and property type count once.
  • Ranges: implausible values are removed and the interquartile range (p25–p75) is published. Only with 20 units or more.
